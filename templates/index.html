What is Euler's Method?

Euler's Method is a simple numerical method used to solve first-order Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) with a given initial value. It works by approximating the unknown curve of a function step-by-step, using the slope (derivative) at a known point to find the value of the next point.

Consider a first-order differential equation expressed as:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y)$$

Given the initial starting values \( (x_0, y_0) \) and a constant step size \(h\), we can approximate the next value \(y_{n+1}\) using the standard Euler's method formula:

$$y_{n+1} = y_n + h \cdot f(x_n, y_n)$$

Step-by-step Solution using Euler's Method:

Step 1: Standardize the Differential Equation
Ensure that the given first-order differential equation is isolated and written clearly in the form of:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y)$$

Step 2: Calculate the Sub-intervals for x
Determine all the x values from the initial point \( x_0 \) up to the target value \( x_{\text{target}} \) using the constant step size \( h \):

$$x_{n+1} = x_n + h$$

This generates the sequence of points: \(x_0, x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\).

Step 3: Iterative Calculation for y_n+1
Solve for the corresponding y values step-by-step using the Euler's formula:

$$y_{n+1} = y_n + h \cdot f(x_n, y_n)$$

Substitute the values of \(x\) and \(y\) from the previous step. Repeat this process until you reach \(x_{n-1}\) to find the final approximated value of \(y\) at the target \(x_n\).

Examples

Example 1:

Problem:
Approximate the value of \( y(3) \) with a step size of \( h = 0.5 \), given the initial condition \( (0,1) \) and the differential equation:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} - x = y$$

Solution:

Step 1: Standardize the Differential Equation
We need to move \( -x \) to the right side to isolate \( \frac{dy}{dx} \):

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = x + y$$

Now, our standard function is defined as \( f(x, y) = x + y \).

Step 2: Identify the Sub-intervals for x
Starting from \( x_0 = 0 \) up to \( x = 3 \) with \( h = 0.5 \), we get the sequence:

  • \( x_0 = 0 \)
  • \( x_1 = 0.5 \)
  • \( x_2 = 1.0 \)
  • \( x_3 = 1.5 \)
  • \( x_4 = 2.0 \)
  • \( x_5 = 2.5 \)
  • \( x_6 = 3.0 \) (Target Value)

Step 3: Iterative Calculation
We apply the formula \( y_{n+1} = y_n + h \cdot (x_n + y_n) \) step-by-step:

Iteration 1 (to find \( y_1 \) at \( x_1 = 0.5 \)):

$$y_1 = 1 + 0.5 \cdot (0 + 1) = 1.5$$

Iteration 2 (to find \( y_2 \) at \( x_2 = 1.0 \)):

$$y_2 = 1.5 + 0.5 \cdot (0.5 + 1.5) = 2.5$$

Iteration 3 (to find \( y_3 \) at \( x_3 = 1.5 \)):

$$y_3 = 2.5 + 0.5 \cdot (1.0 + 2.5) = 4.25$$

Iteration 4 (to find \( y_4 \) at \( x_4 = 2.0 \)):

$$y_4 = 4.25 + 0.5 \cdot (1.5 + 4.25) = 7.125$$

Iteration 5 (to find \( y_5 \) at \( x_5 = 2.5 \)):

$$y_5 = 7.125 + 0.5 \cdot (2.0 + 7.125) = 11.6875$$

Iteration 6 (Final step to find \( y_6 \) at \( x_6 = 3.0 \)):

$$y_6 = 11.6875 + 0.5 \cdot (2.5 + 11.6875) = 18.78125$$

\( \therefore \) The approximated value of \( y(3) \) is 18.78125.

Example 2:

Problem:
Approximate the value of \( y(6) \) with a step size of \( h = 1.2 \) given the initial condition \( (0, 1) \). The first-order differential equation is given in standard form:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos(x) - 0.2y$$

Solution:

Step 1: Identify the Standard Function
The equation is already isolated on the left side, so our standard function is:

$$f(x, y) = \cos(x) - 0.2y$$

Step 2: Identify the Sub-intervals for x
Starting from \( x_0 = 0 \) up to the target \( x = 6 \) with a step size of \( h = 1.2 \), we generate the following points:

  • \( x_0 = 0 \)
  • \( x_1 = 1.2 \)
  • \( x_2 = 2.4 \)
  • \( x_3 = 3.6 \)
  • \( x_4 = 4.8 \)
  • \( x_5 = 6.0 \) (Target Value)

Step 3: Iterative Calculation using Euler's Formula
We apply the standard formula \( y_{n+1} = y_n + h \cdot f(x_n, y_n) \) step-by-step (with your scientific calculator set to Radian mode):

Iteration 1 (to find \( y_1 \) at \( x_1 = 1.2 \)):

$$y_1 = y_0 + h \cdot \left( \cos(x_0) - 0.2y_0 \right)$$ $$y_1 = 1 + 1.2 \cdot \left( \cos(0) - 0.2(1) \right)$$ $$y_1 = 1 + 1.2 \cdot (1 - 0.2) = 1 + 1.2(0.8) = 1.9600$$

Iteration 2 (to find \( y_2 \) at \( x_2 = 2.4 \)):

$$y_2 = y_1 + h \cdot \left( \cos(x_1) - 0.2y_1 \right)$$ $$y_2 = 1.9600 + 1.2 \cdot \left( \cos(1.2) - 0.2(1.9600) \right)$$ $$y_2 = 1.9600 + 1.2 \cdot (0.3624 - 0.3920) = 1.9600 + 1.2 \cdot (-0.0296) = 1.9245$$

Iteration 3 (to find \( y_3 \) at \( x_3 = 3.6 \)):

$$y_3 = y_2 + h \cdot \left( \cos(x_2) - 0.2y_2 \right)$$ $$y_3 = 1.9245 + 1.2 \cdot \left( \cos(2.4) - 0.2(1.9245) \right)$$ $$y_3 = 1.9245 + 1.2 \cdot (-0.7374 - 0.3849) = 1.9245 + 1.2 \cdot (-1.1223) = 0.5777$$

Iteration 4 (to find \( y_4 \) at \( x_4 = 4.8 \)):

$$y_4 = y_3 + h \cdot \left( \cos(x_3) - 0.2y_3 \right)$$ $$y_4 = 0.5777 + 1.2 \cdot \left( \cos(3.6) - 0.2(0.5777) \right)$$ $$y_4 = 0.5777 + 1.2 \cdot (-0.8968 - 0.1155) = 0.5777 + 1.2 \cdot (-1.0123) = -0.6371$$

Iteration 5 (Final step to find \( y_5 \) at \( x_5 = 6.0 \)):

$$y_5 = y_4 + h \cdot \left( \cos(x_4) - 0.2y_4 \right)$$ $$y_5 = -0.6371 + 1.2 \cdot \left( \cos(4.8) - 0.2(-0.6371) \right)$$ $$y_5 = -0.6371 + 1.2 \cdot (0.0875 + 0.1274) = -0.6371 + 1.2 \cdot (0.2149) = -0.3792$$

Final Answer:
\( \therefore \) The approximated value of \( y(6) \) using Euler's Method is -0.3792.
